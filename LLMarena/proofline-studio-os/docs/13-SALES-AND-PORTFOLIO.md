# 13 — Sales, Portfolio and Outbound (without fake proof)

## Portfolio information architecture
`/work` → three groups, in this order: **(1) Concept work (labelled) · (2) Redesign studies (labelled) · (3) Client work (permissioned)**. Each card states class, direction used, and the constraint it explores. `/work/master-site` is the flagship: our own site documented as a build with receipts.

## Case-study structure (template: `templates/case-study.md`)
`Context (1 sentence) → Constraint (what made it hard) → Approach (the gates used) → Artefacts (screens, tokens, receipts) → Outcome (measured, client-reported, or "not measured") → Disclosure line`.
Length: 400–700 words. No adjectives doing the work of evidence.

## 30-prospect research workflow (one afternoon per week)
1. Define the slice: e.g. "AI automation consultants, 1–15 people, English site, launched >6 months ago, visible offer mismatch".
2. Collect 30 names with URL + the *specific* observation (max 25 words) that makes them a fit.
3. Score each 0–2 on five axes → total /10:
   - Offer clarity problem visible on the page
   - Has real evidence (case notes, client names, demos) they aren't using well
   - Can afford €500–3,000 without a procurement cycle
   - Decision maker reachable (founder/partner, not a gatekeeper)
   - Site is young enough to still be a decision, old enough to be measured
4. Prioritise ≥8/10. Send ≤5 personalised messages per day — quality over volume.
5. Track: sent → replied → teardown sent → call → proposal → won/lost, with the *reason* recorded.

## Outreach pattern (teardown-led, no pitch in message one)
1. **Observation** — one specific, correct, non-insulting thing you noticed (with a screenshot if helpful).
2. **Consequence** — why it probably costs them (in visitor behaviour terms, not in invented numbers).
3. **Offer** — "I do a 3-point teardown of one page; it takes me 45 minutes. Want it? No call needed."
4. **Proof posture** — link the public teardown method + concept work, not testimonials you don't have.
5. **Exit** — one line that makes refusal easy.

Rules: no mass templates, no fake familiarity, no "I loved your website" filler, no follow-up more than twice, unsubscribe instantly on request. Never send an unauthorised teardown of a *person's* private data or unlaunched page.

## Pricing presentation
Show the three tiers with **what is excluded**, the delivery window, the revision count and the ownership model on the same screen. Anchor with the middle tier. Never discount the price silently — reduce scope explicitly instead.

## Objection handling (short forms)
| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "Too expensive." | Reduce scope to Starter; the gates stay identical. |
| "I can do this with an AI builder." | "You can. You get speed; what you don't get is the licence audit, the accessibility sweep, the performance budget or someone who signs a QA receipt." |
| "Can you guarantee conversions?" | "No. Anyone who does is selling you a story. I can guarantee clarity, accessibility, performance and honest measurement." |
| "We need it in 3 days." | "Then it's Starter scope, 5 days, and I'll tell you exactly what won't be included." |
| "Can you make it look like <brand>?" | "No. I'll make it look like *you*, with references to patterns — never to another brand's identity." |

## Proof collection after projects (the learning loop)
At handover + 30 days, request: a verbatim quote, permission to publish, and 2–3 numbers the client is willing to stand behind. Log the permission, add the case study, and update `docs/02` with whatever the project taught about messaging. One new reusable artefact per project, maximum.
