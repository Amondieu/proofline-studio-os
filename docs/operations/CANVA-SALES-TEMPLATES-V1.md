# Canva sales and delivery templates V1

<!-- v0.1 · 2026-09-23 · owner: founder · status: build later -->

This is the production order for Proofline Studio’s small Canva layer. Canva
packages thinking for sales and handover; it does not build or version the
website, define design tokens, run QA, or establish asset rights.

## Template order

| Priority | Template | Moment | Job |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `01-3-Point-Landing-Page-Teardown` | Outreach / first conversation | Demonstrate specific thinking before a sales call |
| 2 | `02-Authority-Sprint-Proposal` | After Discovery | Make scope, price, process, and boundaries concrete |
| 3 | `03-Creative-Direction-Board` | Before Build | Get one direction selected before production |
| 4 | `04-Case-Study-Project-Reveal` | After launch | Tell a verified decision story, not a screenshot story |
| 5 | `05-Client-Launch-Kit` | Handover | Help the client communicate the new site |

Build priorities 1 and 3 first. They connect directly to the teardown-led
sales path and the existing three-direction gate.

## Template 1 — 3-point teardown

Format: 6–8 slides, 16:9 PDF or presentation.

```text
1. [Company] landing-page teardown
2. What a first-time visitor currently understands
3. Leak 1 — unclear offer, audience, or outcome
4. Leak 2 — weak or hidden conversion path
5. Leak 3 — missing proof or risk reduction
6. Proposed hero rewrite
7. Suggested conversion architecture
8. Recommended next step: [specific sprint]
```

Rules:

- describe only observable leaks;
- do not claim conversion loss without verified data;
- do not invent benchmarks, logos, testimonials, or screenshots;
- label independent concepts and unaffiliated redesign studies;
- link the teardown to the candidate archetype as a hypothesis, not a fact.

## Template 2 — Authority Sprint proposal

Format: 10–12 slides or PDF.

```text
1. Project context
2. Decision the page must solve
3. Target buyer and conversion goal
4. Recommended archetype
5. Recommended creative direction
6. Included scope
7. Excluded scope
8. Timeline and client responsibilities
9. Investment and payment terms
10. QA, ownership, and handover
11. Revision policy
12. Decision: accept or ask for clarification
```

The archetype and direction remain recommendations until Discovery and the
relevant client approvals are recorded.

## Template 3 — Creative Direction Board

Format: one wide board or four slides.

```text
1. Direction name and buyer emotion
2. Palette and typography
3. Layout and grid
4. Image and motion treatment
5. CTA treatment
6. Do / Don’t
7. Accessibility and performance constraints
8. Approval block
```

Create three master variants:

| Direction | Suitable starting archetypes |
| --- | --- |
| Precision System | AI / Automation Authority, SaaS Launch & Demand |
| Cinematic Authority | SaaS Launch & Demand, Creative Portfolio & Inquiry |
| Editorial Luxury | Expert Authority, High-Trust Lead Engine |

This is a starting recommendation only. The repository `DesignDirection` record
and client selection remain authoritative.

## Template 4 — Case study / project reveal

Format: 8–10 carousel slides or PDF.

```text
1. Business challenge
2. Costly visitor confusion
3. Message decision
4. Creative direction
5. Conversion architecture
6. Mobile, performance, or accessibility decision
7. Final system
8. Outcome — verified data only
9. Learning
10. CTA
```

If verified client outcomes do not exist, use:

> Studio Concept — independent demonstration. No client affiliation and no
> performance outcome is implied.

## Template 5 — Client launch kit

Format: 5–8 editable social or sales assets.

```text
1. New website announcement
2. What changed / what the client now offers
3. Who it is for
4. Main service or use case
5. Genuine social proof only
6. CTA to the website
```

## Proofline Canva kit

Create one internal Proofline kit, not a separate uncontrolled system per
client:

```text
Proofline Studio

Direction 01 — Precision System
Direction 02 — Cinematic Authority
Direction 03 — Editorial Luxury

Folders
01 — Teardowns
02 — Proposals
03 — Direction Boards
04 — Case Studies
05 — Client Launch Kits
```

Keep logos, palettes, fonts, and templates versioned against the repository
direction records. Canva is a presentation surface; the repository retains the
design-system authority.

## Rights and delivery rules

- Use client logos, photos, testimonials, and screenshots only with documented
  permission.
- Register each production asset in the repository asset-rights record.
- Check Canva content and template licensing before commercial delivery or an
  editable handoff.
- Deliver a PDF export by default; provide an editable link only after the
  asset and template rights are reviewed.
- Never resell another creator’s template as Proofline’s own system.
- Do not present AI-generated people as real staff, clients, or testimonials.
- Do not promise exclusivity or trademark transfer without legal review.

## Pilot acceptance test

1. Produce one teardown for the Proofline master site.
2. Produce one direction board containing all three directions.
3. Confirm every statement is sourced, labelled, or explicitly hypothetical.
4. Confirm PDF exports are readable and usable without an editable link.
5. Record all non-owned assets and rights decisions.
6. Link the final artifact to the relevant project, archetype, and approval.
7. Hand the package to a second reviewer before using it for a prospect.
